The ocean is one of the most important natural features on Earth. It covers more than 70 percent of the planet’s surface and contains most of Earth’s water. Because of its size, the ocean affects climate, weather, ecosystems, food supplies, transport, and human life. Although people often think of land as the centre of human activity, the ocean quietly supports life in many ways.

One important fact about the ocean is that it helps regulate Earth’s climate. Water can absorb and store large amounts of heat. Another important role of the ocean is producing oxygen. Many people associate oxygen mainly with trees and forests, but microscopic ocean plants called phytoplankton also produce oxygen through photosynthesis. These organisms use sunlight, carbon dioxide, and water to create energy, releasing oxygen as a result. This shows that the ocean is not just a body of water; it is also a living system that contributes to the air we breathe.

The ocean is also important for trade and transport. For centuries, humans have used seas and oceans to travel, exchange goods, explore new places, and connect societies. Today, many products are transported by ships across the world. This makes the ocean a key part of the global economy. Coastal areas are also important for tourism, recreation, and cultural identity.

However, the ocean faces serious threats. Pollution, overfishing, habitat destruction, and climate change can damage marine ecosystems. Plastic waste is especially harmful because it can be eaten by marine animals or break into tiny pieces called microplastics. Rising temperatures can also affect coral reefs and change the habitats of many ocean species.
